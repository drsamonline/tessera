PolyForm Noncommercial is designed for exactly this: it restricts *users*, not the licensor, so the copyright holder may
offer the same code under different terms. Nothing about the free licence changes, and nothing already released becomes
retroactively restricted.

## What a commercial licence gives you

- The right to use, modify and deploy TESSERA in a commercial setting, including as part of a product you sell.
- The right to keep your modifications private. The noncommercial licence already permits modification; a commercial
  licence makes that useful to a business.
- A written statement of terms you can show your own legal and procurement teams.

## What it does not give you

- **No exclusivity.** The same terms are available to anyone.
- **No trademark rights.** "TESSERA" as a mark is not licensed. A common English word is hard to protect and harder to
  police; if you need a brand, use your own.
- **No warranty.** The software is provided as is, exactly as the free licence says. See the warranty disclaimer in
  [LICENSE](LICENSE).
- **No support commitment** unless a separate agreement says so.

## Why not simply go permissive

MIT or Apache would let anyone sell this without paying, including a competitor with a larger sales team. A permissive
licence on a product whose only moat is its engineering is a donation.

## Why not go fully proprietary

The credibility of this repository rests on a reader being able to check the claims: 748 tests, a generated conformance
report, a claim register with four rows marked *not verified*. A closed licence would make all of that unverifiable, and
unverifiable evidence is marketing.

## What a lawyer must confirm before you rely on this

- Whether the dual-licensing structure is enforceable as written in your jurisdiction.
- Whether the PolyForm Noncommercial terms cover your intended free tier — the boundary between "internal trial" and
  "commercial use" is narrower than it looks.
- Whether "TESSERA" is registrable or defensible as a mark in your classes, and what to do about the many existing uses
  of the word.
